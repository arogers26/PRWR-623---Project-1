# Overview

One major aspect of grant writing is organization because grant writers need to be aware of grant requirements, proposal status, application deadlines, and information about funders. Grant tracking documentation is vital because it organizes all crucial information into one source. 

Without proper documentation to store all grant details, students are more prone to overlook or forget key elements of their grant. Forgetting, losing, or misplacing information pertaining to your grant, can be catastrophic for your proposal submission. Grant writing is a detail-oriented profession. Therefore, it is paramount to keep record of all grant guidelines and requirements. If you do not abide by these regulations or do not attach needed documentation, funders are less likely to choose your proposal and you will not receive program funding.

# Defining Terminology
These are some definitions to provide context for the document.
* Organization Applicant: The person who is applying for a grant/funding on behalf of a nonprofit. 
* Grantor: The individual who is in charge of funding opportunites and oversees all grant submissions.
* RFPs (Request for Proposals): A funder's/foundation's request that asks nonprofits to apply for funding. 
* LOI (Letter of Inquiry): Before submitting a grant, some funders may require an LOI. This is shorter than a grant proposal. It introduces funders to the nonprofit you are writing for and allows them to learn a brief summary of your organization's mission/purpose. Funders examine LOIs and notify you if they want you to submit a full grant proposal or not.
* Rolling Deadline: The deadline is ongoing and does not have a specified end date.
